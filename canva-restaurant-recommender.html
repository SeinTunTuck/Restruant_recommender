Problem and Motivation

Restaurant ranking needs more than popularity.

  • Users have different cuisine and budget preferences.
  • Distance and constraints affect real dining decisions.
  • Rating-only systems can miss important context.
  • A selectable method helps users compare outcomes.
  • Transparent ranking reasons improve trust.
Dataset and Inputs

Data combines users, venues, and ratings.

User dataProfiles, cuisine preferences, payment preferences, and anonymous matched user history.
Restaurant dataLocation, price, cuisine, parking, accessibility, alcohol, hours, and payment options.
Rating dataOverall rating, food rating, and service rating used as behavioral evidence.
Web App Workflow

From dining brief to ranked shortlist.

1. User completes dining brief
2. App matches an anonymous profile
3. User selects recommendation method
4. Results update instantly
5. Restaurants can be compared side by side
Method 1

Item-Based Collaborative Filtering

  • Compares restaurants instead of users.
  • Uses shared user-rating patterns.
  • Recommends venues similar to liked restaurants.
  • Works best when enough rating history exists.
  • Less dependent on restaurant attributes.
Method 2

Location-Aware Recommendation

  • Uses distance from the selected dining area.
  • Rewards nearby restaurants.
  • Still respects cuisine, budget, and constraints.
  • Useful for real-world restaurant decisions.
  • Can work even with sparse rating history.
Method 3

Hybrid Recommendation

QualityBayesian-adjusted overall, food, and service ratings.
TasteItem-based and user-based collaborative signals.
ContextPreference fit plus travel convenience.

Evidence 1

Composite rating calculation

Composite Rating = 0.50R + 0.30F + 0.20S R = overall rating, F = food rating, S = service rating
Example

If R = 2, F = 1, S = 2, then score = 0.50(2) + 0.30(1) + 0.20(2) = 1.70 out of 2.

Why it matters

This creates a single score for each rating record while still keeping food and service as separate evidence.

Evidence 2

Item-Based CF similarity calculation

Similarity(A,B) = (A · B) / (||A|| × ||B||) A and B are restaurant rating vectors from shared users.
Prediction

Predicted score = weighted average of restaurants the matched diner already rated, weighted by item similarity.

Evidence signal

The app counts how many similar restaurant signals support each recommendation.

Evidence 3

Location-aware calculation

Convenience = 1 - distance / max(radius × 1.15, 2) Distance is calculated from latitude and longitude using Haversine distance.
Example

With an 8 km radius and a restaurant 2 km away: convenience = 1 - 2 / 9.2 = 0.78.

Method score

Location score = 58% convenience + 25% preference fit + 17% rating quality.

Evidence 4

Hybrid recommendation score

Final Score = 100 × (Qwq + Twt + Fwf + Cwc) Q = quality, T = collaborative taste, F = preference fit, C = convenience.
Default weights

Quality 34%, Taste 28%, Fit 24%, Convenience 14%.

Example

If Q=.80, T=.70, F=.90, C=.78, final score = 100 × (.272 + .196 + .216 + .109) = 79.3%.

Conclusion

Hybrid gives the strongest decision support.

  • Item-Based CF captures behavioral similarity.
  • Location-Aware recommendation captures practical context.
  • Hybrid combines behavior, context, and explicit preferences.
  • User choice makes the comparison visible.
  • Future work can add evaluation metrics and live feedback.
